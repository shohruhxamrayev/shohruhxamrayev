Before the Coffee Gets Cold

Toshikazu Kawaguchi

Read: April 28, 2026 • Rating: 6/10

I am going to be honest here, the only reason why I grabbed this book is its title. Yeah, nothing else. Just the title.

Do you have regrets? Maybe you have lost someone and you really want to meet with them one last time. Perhaps you want to change one thing in the past that could potentially prevent your future hurts. I mean, we are simple yet complicated (at the same time) human beings. We want a lot sometimes. We regret. We lose. We want people back. We want a change. We want to do better next time. Why is it so hard to accept what is happening to us? Why are we predisposed to be so stubborn? Why do we try to increase our capacity of acceptance? Would one change in the past could alter our present or future in any way? If not, why do we keep asking "I wish it could have been that way, this way, the other way".

This is a harsh truth, isn't it?! We are in a "system" called destiny that our past is unchangeable and our future is completely unknown. We only have our present in our hands. Yet we are still out of much control. But we still try to control sometimes. We get upset about what fate has given us. We get upset about what fate has taken from us. Feels a bit unfair?!

I assume that the theory behind this book is that people who could have gone back to past cannot change the present, but they can change how they see things, how they think about their own life and decisions. It also tells me somethings that has been cliche honestly. They change how they think about certain situations because in their past, they had regrets regarding those actions. Therefore, we try not to do things we would regret in the future, and we should not regret what we have done in the past. The book does a good job explaining this clear truth.

You may wonder, why is there "coffee" in the title? Then, you would want to read the book before your coffee gets cold.
